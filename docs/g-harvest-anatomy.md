# G corpse harvest anatomy

Holding G over an eligible dead zombie now splits and retracts the actual imported torso mesh. `HarvestSurface` clips its private source triangles at the incision and gives the two sides separate seam vertices. New vertices interpolate the original UVs, normals and material attributes; bone influences are merged and normalized rather than interpolating bone indices. The original skinned mesh, jacket texture and skeleton remain in use. A front-only torso membership filter leaves the back, limbs and head intact. The former oval fragment-discard hole and synthetic skin flaps are disabled for this model.

The incision advances from chest to abdomen with the blade. Source vertices retract laterally and lift at an uneven cut edge, exposing heart, paired lungs, liver, stomach, kidneys, spleen, colon, small intestines and curved tapered ribs seated 7.5 cm inside the source skin plane. Three varying-width surfaces expose dermis, fat and fascia along the actual source seam. The skin frame copies the final chest/abdomen pose after ragdoll synchronization. Deformation is mapped back through each vertex's inverse weighted skin transform, so the original skeleton still supplies the posed result and all shadow/depth passes see the same physical opening. The shared GLB, gameplay positions and corpse collision proxies remain unchanged.

The existing draw/incise/rip clocks and reward gates remain authoritative. Releasing G retains the partial physical incision without awarding a harvest. Completed bodies retain exposed anatomy after the foreground effect ends. The heart becomes a fitted rigid drop in the existing Cannon world. The intestine is one continuous procedural tube following a 30-particle constrained chain, with distributed compliant mesentery support, a torso anchor and a short sideways spring-guided spill. The end releases before the heart lift finishes. Its guide updates a force target during physics ticks; render updates never teleport the dynamic endpoint. Legacy hard guides remain the default for other physics callers.

Rendering is bounded to four dressed corpses. The anatomy allocation uses 9,896 triangles, 13 meshes and five fallback materials; three substitute skin/rim meshes stay hidden for the imported zombie. Generated atlas material variants borrow two shared 1024-square textures. The tested source model has 31,905 source triangles, adds 216–218 triangles at the cut depending on the corpse pose, and registers 432–436 seam endpoints and about 6,200 affected vertices. One additional draw supplies cut-edge thickness. Unchanged skinning/opening skips source uploads, and conservative geometry bounds avoid full source scans during updates. Harvested corpses are prioritized inside the twelve-actor wound pool. Cosmetic physics permits two intestine chains globally, at most 32 particles per chain, 60 Hz steps, static floor/wall contact, 4 m/s tissue velocity limits, 55 m/s² combined support/guide acceleration and a bounded extension guard. The tissue intentionally does not collide with its own packed coils or enclosing ragdoll proxies. This is constrained particle tissue, not a volumetric soft-body solver.

Gore-off hides all anatomy and organ drops and restores the exact source positions and normals. Switching it on restores the stored incision. Reduced motion suppresses incidental heart motion while retaining the cut and physics. Restart, course changes, corpse removal and pool eviction restore the private source buffers and remove owned geometry, organ bodies, particle constraints and cut registration. Runtime/build require only the repository's existing Node and bundled Three.js/Cannon assets.

Implementation ownership:

- `extraction-surface.js`: original-model seam topology, interpolated skinning/UVs, source retraction, actual edge thickness and buffer restoration.
- `extraction-anatomy.js`: seeded parametric organ surfaces, rib/colon sweeps, dynamic intestine tube and allocation cleanup.
- `extraction-cutaway.js`: persistent corpse dressing, socket frames, extraction endpoint and heart handoff.
- `extraction-vfx.js`: G phase presentation and world-space cutting tools.
- `extraction-tissue-materials.js`: shared image/height ownership, UV parallax, derivative normals, wet roughness, and bounded incoming light.
- `brutal-combat-visuals.js`: private source geometry ownership and retained harvested actors; other combat cut shaders remain independent.
- `corpse-physics.js`: bounded chains sharing the existing corpse/drop world.
- `renderer.js`: extraction presentation after the corpse physics update.
- `build.mjs`: explicit inclusion of the four extraction runtime modules.
