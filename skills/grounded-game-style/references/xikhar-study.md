interiors or collision exist in an interactive 3D runtime. The screenshots
also cannot establish continuous motion quality or performance.

## Adaptation for Blackwater

Keep the existing N64-inspired horror-survival contract: coarse silhouettes,
low-resolution surface information, controlled color bands, readable hands and
weapons, and a restrained field-equipment interface. Do not replace it with the
clip's realistic medieval architecture or its presentation-page layout.

- Use terrain and connected streets to explain districts. Retaining walls,
  foundations, steps and paths should agree with the actual walking surface.
- Give settlement clusters a clear center and a landmark; preserve useful
  negative space around entrances, combat lanes and scavenging targets.
- Keep adjacent homes in related roof/wall/material families with purposeful
  variation. Kitchens, sleeping spaces and storage should support the house's
  use and leave a continuous player route.
- Place foliage in coherent edges, groves and maintained/broken boundaries.
  Avoid filling every gap with identical evenly scattered props.
- Translate the reference's hierarchy into concise game typography, one
  interaction emphasis and consistent spacing. Keep the established moss,
  parchment and brass field palette; the clip's orange is not a mandatory HUD
  color. Avoid floating dashboard cards and explanatory website copy in play.
- Compare first-person weapons against the world at normal play size. Surface
  richness must not conceal grip, silhouette, contact, recoil or return.

Suggested initial specimen: one island home from approach through doorway,
interior search, weapon pickup and combat, followed by a city block connected
to a visible landmark. Validate the actual route before expanding its pattern.

## Evidence discipline

Record which frames came from a reference, an image-generation experiment,
held construction, an observer camera, native local play or the deployed game.
Keep the same camera for before/after claims. A polished reference image is a
target; acceptance belongs to the implemented playable scene.
